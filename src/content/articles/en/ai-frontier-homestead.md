## You're already throwing away ore. Nobody told you it was ore.

If your daily LINE chats, customer questions, quotes, and class notes are written once and then discarded, you're throwing away ore every day. This article is about how to dig it out with AI and refine it into assets that keep bringing in income and keep growing. You don't need to code, and you don't need to buy any new tool first.

> <img src="/images/dock_head_s.png" alt="Duck Editor" width="24" style="vertical-align: middle;"> **Duck Editor's note**: The biggest waste in the AI era isn't paying for tools. It's that your experience and data never get kept. Tools come and go; the data and methods you've organized are your ore.

## The frontier: you're standing on a Moon nobody has finished building

Imagine you've been sent to a lunar colony. There's ore under the ground, and supply crates scattered across the surface: free tools, trial credits, small open-source programs. Some people pick up supplies and dig all the way to treasure. Some get a faster mining rig first. Some stand still and watch everyone else work.

I'm not trying to scare you. I'm reminding you that AI tools are cheap and easy to pick up right now, which is exactly the time to plant your flag early.

![In the AI era, an individual is like a frontier settler: picking up free tool supplies, mining, building a base, and upgrading along the way](/images/articles/ai-frontier-homestead/ai-frontier-pioneer.jpg)

But the crates change. Free plans often get adjusted, and a credit you have today may shrink tomorrow. So don't chase the supplies themselves. Chase this instead: **whether the ore you already have is actually being dug out.**

## Step 1: Survey. List your veins first.

The first step in mining isn't digging; it's surveying. Take a sheet of paper and write down what you produce every day and handle over and over.

The four examples below are made-up scenarios for illustration only, not real cases. They're here to show you how to take inventory:

- **Imagine a salesperson**: client correspondence, quotes you've already sent, the terms clients keep asking about, and which clients come back to buy again.
- **Imagine a department manager**: weekly meeting notes, tasks you've delegated and their progress, and the same questions your team keeps asking.
- **Imagine a beauty salon manager (service industry)**: common complaints and how you replied, regulars' preferences, and which time slots always end up overbooked.
- **Imagine the owner of a small goods shop (retail)**: stock-in and stock-on-hand records, customers asking about sizes or whether something is in stock, which products sell best, and how customers react when something sells out.

These look like small things, but they're all veins. Your ore is probably hiding in the category you pay the least attention to.

## Step 2: Choose. Three questions tell you what's worth digging.

Not every rock is valuable. Take each item from your survey and ask yourself three questions:

1. Do I keep doing this over and over?
2. Would someone pay for this knowledge, or save time because of it?
3. Once it's organized, can I reuse it again and again?

If two or more answers are "yes," it's worth digging. If you can't answer, set it aside for now.

## Step 3: Build the rig. Start by hand; you don't need to get there in one step.

Many people hear "AI automation" and back off, thinking it takes technical skill. In reality, mining rigs come in three levels, and you can upgrade one at a time:

- **Manual**: Paste your material into an AI and ask it to organize it into a table, key points, or a reply draft. You can do this today, with no setup.
- **Semi-automatic**: Write your usual way of doing things as a fixed prompt, or set up a small dedicated assistant that remembers your rules. For example, a manager dictates the key points after each meeting, and the AI turns them into meeting notes and an action-item list. (For how to write fixed instructions, see [How to Set a System Prompt](/en/articles/set-system-prompt/). For feeding context by speaking, see [Voice Input for AI Context](/en/articles/voice-input-ai-context/).)
- **Automated**: Let an AI agent do work on a schedule. For example, a salesperson's assistant sorts new client inquiries each week and drafts quote replies, and the salesperson only does the final check. This level takes more setup, so wait until the first two steps feel comfortable.

The rule is simple: **pick the most annoying, most repetitive task, and move it from manual to semi-automatic. That alone saves a lot of time.**

## Step 4: Refine. Turn what you dug up into assets.

Ore has to be refined before it's worth anything. "Activating an asset" means turning what's sitting in your phone, your computer, and your head into something that keeps creating value:

- The salesperson turns past quotes and client correspondence into a template library, so the next quote doesn't start from scratch. In effect, capacity goes up.
- The manager turns meeting notes into a delegation template, so follow-ups after each meeting are less likely to slip through the cracks.
- The salon manager turns common complaints and standard replies into a script list, so new staff get up to speed faster and customers don't have to wait as long.
- The shop owner turns stock-in and stock-on-hand records into restocking rules, so they can order ahead and run out of stock less often.

These are imagined scenarios, but the point is the same: **organize it once, and use it every time after.**

## The strategy game mindset: level up yourself, and raise your assistant too

It helps to think of this as a strategy simulation game. You have three tracks to run at the same time:

1. **Level up your own skills.** You need to judge what a good answer looks like, know when to stop and check, and know which things should never be handed to AI. There's no shortcut for this track, though AI can help you learn faster.
2. **Build your skills into your workflow.** Write the methods you've figured out into fixed prompts or steps, so they stop being experience that lives only in your head.
3. **Make your assistant stronger.** The tidier your data and the clearer your rules, the better your AI assistant understands you, and the less you have to explain from scratch each time.

The last point is the one to remember: **tools change, but the data and workflows you build stay.** That's why you don't need to chase every new tool ([Don't Let FOMO Drive Your AI Tool Choices](/en/articles/dont-fomo-ai-tools/)), and you don't need to pick "the best one" on day one ([Which AI Tool Is for You?](/en/articles/which-ai-tool-for-you/)).

## 🚨 Three common traps

- **Trying to build the whole base at once.** You end up finishing nothing. Build one room first, then add the next one when it's done.
- **Pasting customers' or staff's personal details or passwords straight in.** Before you upload, hide names, phone numbers, account numbers, and passwords. Also check how the service you're using handles data, rather than relying on your memory of it. ([A Share Link Is Not Private](/en/articles/ai-share-link-not-private/))
- **Digging the ore but not saving it.** Many people close the chat when they're done and start from zero next time. Save your useful prompts and organized material into your own notes or documents, and that's when it really accumulates. ([Personal Knowledge Management](/en/articles/pkm-system/) can serve as the storage structure.)

## The settler's mindset: plant the flag first, then build slowly

The most important thing in Moon settlement is planting your flag early. You don't need to finish the whole base at once. Survey one thing today, make it semi-automatic tomorrow, add one more the day after, and the mine grows a little every day.

When that happens, you'll notice you're no longer just a user of AI. You're the owner of this mine.

## One thing you can do tomorrow

Grab your phone or a notebook and spend 15 minutes on these three things:

1. Write down one task you've done at least three times this week, such as answering the same question or formatting the same kind of quote.
2. Open the AI you normally use and describe the task to it: how you do it each time, what material it needs, and what the final output looks like.
3. Ask it to turn this into a "fixed set of steps," and save that in your notes. Next time the same task comes up, paste those steps in.

That's your first mining rig. You don't have to install anything, and you can start today.
